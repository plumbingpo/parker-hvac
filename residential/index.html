No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Before we start any repair, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your system's age and tonnage, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get an honest recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is swapping a weak capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

The problem with HVAC failures is that most of them develop where you can't see them. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down gradually, and a weak capacitor lasts right up until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. When bills climb, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most air conditioners and furnaces last 12 to 18 years, and a planned replacement is always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap during a heat wave or cold snap. Our technicians know the climate demands common to Parker and how they affect equipment life, so we can tell you honestly whether your system has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Seasonal HVAC Maintenance Plans

Each maintenance visit from Parker HVAC is far more than a quick filter swap. Our technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, puts in a fresh filter, and measures static pressure across the ducts. Small problems are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the equipment stays running clean and efficient.

Many homes do best with two visits a year: a springtime tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. We adjust the schedule to the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and local conditions like dust and pollen, which coat condenser coils and raise energy use fast.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule reduces operating costs. A clean coil transfers heat far more efficiently, a properly charged system stops overworking the compressor, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing an HVAC system even one year early.

Proper Sizing

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

An undersized system runs nonstop and never catches up on the hottest days, while an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and leaves the house humid. Parker HVAC sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual home. That means a proper load calculation that accounts for square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout, rather than simply matching the tonnage of the old unit. For ductless mini-splits, it means matching head capacity to each zone so every room holds its setpoint.

Picking a new system comes down to more than a SEER2 number. Our technician will compare heat pumps against a furnace-and-AC pair, and ducted against ductless, laying out operating costs, electrical and venting work, and the installation itself. Growing families often do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds more even temperatures across the whole house and uses less energy. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Parker

Most service calls we take in Parker come down to a handful of familiar problems. The air conditioner blows warm air. The furnace won't light, or it lights and shuts off minutes later. The evaporator coil freezes solid, the condenser hums without starting, or a mini-split starts flashing error codes. Add refrigerant leaks, clogged drains, and forgetful thermostats, and you have the full list. Our technicians handle all of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A bad capacitor only keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a backed-up condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling before anyone notices. We come prepared to replace capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, seal ductwork that's leaking air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak is fixed.
